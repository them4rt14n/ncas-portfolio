About me

I'm Marvin Segismundo, a first-generation college student at City College of San Francisco, or CCSF. I want to be an aerospace design engineer for Advanced Air Mobility, the new kind of flying built around electric vertical takeoff and landing aircraft, or eVTOL. I want to help design the parts and structures that make these aircraft light, safe, and possible to build.

This goal didn't come out of nowhere. I spent more than two years on the ramp and in the cargo office at San Francisco International Airport, loading planes and seeing how much work goes into every single flight. Now I'm working on the engineering side. I'm designing my own eVTOL quadplane, Lipad, in SOLIDWORKS. I helped build the solar panel frame on our Engineering Club boat, which took 2nd place among colleges at the California Solar Regatta. And right now my class team is building a robot that launches tennis balls, using MATLAB to plan every shot.

NASA's Advanced Air Mobility mission is working to make this kind of flying part of everyday life.1 O*NET describes aerospace engineers as the people who design, build, and test aircraft,2 and that's the work I want to do. My plan is to transfer to UC Davis or San Jose State University in 2028, apply for NASA internships along the way, and work toward a NASA Aerospace Technologist job or a design job at an eVTOL company.

Target job: Aerospace Engineers, O*NET 17-2011.00.2 At NASA, the Aerospace Technologist family, 0861 series.

NASA work I'm aiming for: Advanced Air Mobility research at Ames, Glenn, and Langley.1,3

Projects

Lipad eVTOL quadplane

My own project · SOLIDWORKS · 2026 to now

Task, action, result

Task: design my own small eVTOL aircraft. Action: I modeled parts in SOLIDWORKS and, with help from an AI tool, sized a 2.5 kg quadplane with a 1.6 m wing and four lift rotors. Result: on paper it hovers on about 300 W and cruises at 18 m/s for about 20 minutes.

Why it matters for NASA

NASA's Revolutionary Vertical Lift Technology project studies a Lift+Cruise concept with the same layout.4 My math showed the same trade NASA faces: hovering takes over twice the power of cruising, so eVTOL aircraft keep hover time short.5

Tennis Roboreturn

Team project · ENGN 10B · Fall 2026

Task, action, result

Task: build a robot for ENGN 10B that rides a wooden rail and launches tennis balls at targets. Action: I help design the launcher, and we use MATLAB to plan the launch angle and speed. Result: the 3D printed chassis rolls on the rail, and we compete on December 18, 2026.

Why it matters for NASA

O*NET says aerospace engineers plan and run tests on models and prototypes.2 We predict where each ball lands, launch it, measure the miss, and adjust, which is the same design and test loop used in flight testing.

Warren truss bridge with an automated gate

Growth Sector Engineering Degree Apprenticeship Pathway · Summer 2026

Task, action, result

Task: build a model bridge with an automated gate in Growth Sector's EDAP program. Action: my team built a Warren truss and wired a gate run by an Arduino, a sensor, and a servo motor. Result: we finished the bridge and the gate together in summer 2026.

Why it matters for NASA

A truss spreads a load across connected members, the same idea inside aircraft wings and fuselage frames. Adding a sensor and a servo also showed me how structures and electronics have to work together, which is what every eVTOL aircraft needs.
